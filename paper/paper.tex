\begin{abstract}
Complex-valued neural networks (CVNNs) are usually motivated by
phase-bearing data, but the phrase ``complex helps'' hides several
different mechanisms: the coordinate representation of the input,
the algebraic prior imposed by complex multiplication, and the
optimization dynamics induced by coupled real/imaginary parameters. We
study these mechanisms at a scale where they can be separated rather
than conflated. Synthetic representation stress tests show that the
complex advantage is conditional: PSK-only IQ data favors the complex
model (0.821 vs 0.333 for magnitude-only), QAM-only inverts that
ranking, mixed PSK+QAM gives a small complex edge, and an unseen
global phase rotation collapses every coordinate-dependent model
unless rotation augmentation is added. Complex-valued models are
therefore best framed as a useful inductive bias when the task's
phase/amplitude geometry matches the model. A feasibility-scale
quantum wavefunction pilot extends the same question outside RF:
phase-aware models solve momentum-from-wavefunction classification
perfectly while magnitude-only remains at chance (1.000 vs 0.250),
but a 1-D potential-inverse task is best matched by an explicit real
Cartesian view (0.462 vs 0.431 for complex), again emphasizing
representation rather than universal complex superiority. The headline
empirical finding is methodological: on a 3-class RadioML 2018.01A subset,
matched-shared-trial hyperparameter selection produces a $22.94$~pp
CVNN-vs-real gap that collapses to $2.46$~pp under independent
per-family selection. Per-step gradient telemetry attributes the
difference to a step-1 \code{head.weight} gradient explosion ---
50--80$\times$ larger for real baselines than complex --- that pushes
a fraction of real-baseline seeds into a dead-ReLU region they cannot
escape. A 2$\times$2 lr-vs-activation disambiguation localizes the
dead-seed threshold primarily to learning rate: at lr $=$ 0.0024 both
\code{crelu} and \code{zrelu} produce $0/9$ dead seeds, and at lr
$=$ 0.0236 both produce dead seeds ($4/9$ on \code{crelu}, $1/9$ on
\code{zrelu}); activation modulates sensitivity at high lr but does
not by itself trigger the failure mode. We therefore read
matched-shared-trial as a robustness stress test, not as the magnitude
of the complex advantage. The resulting paper is a scoped mechanism
study: it asks when, why, and under what invariance assumptions
complex-valued RF models are worth using, and it releases code,
manifests, stress-test plots, telemetry traces, and figure scripts for
every claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Concept and feasibility scope}
\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{What this paper claims and does not claim}

We make six concrete claims, each at a defensible scale and with
explicit reproducibility:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Architectural claim (formal).}
    \code{ComplexConv1d} is phase-equivariant in the bias-free case
    (\Cref{lem:phase-equivariance}); a magnitude-logit head is
    invariant to global phase rotations of its input features. A real
    \code{Conv1d} on stacked $(I, Q)$ channels
    does not get this for free --- it must learn rotation invariance
    from data via a $2\times 2$ channel mix the complex network gets by
    construction.
  \item \textbf{Representation claim.} The complex-vs-real comparison
    is not meaningful unless we also control the coordinate system
    presented to real networks. On PSK-only synthetic RF, phase-aware
    encodings learn while magnitude-only remains at chance; on QAM-only
    data magnitude becomes competitive; on mixed PSK+QAM the complex
    edge is small. We therefore report complex, Cartesian real,
    polar, phase-only, and magnitude-only baselines together
    (\Cref{sec:representation-stress}) rather than treating
    $(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$ stacking as the only real baseline.
  \item \textbf{Application pilot claim.} A 1-D quantum wavefunction
    pilot (\Cref{sec:quantum-pilot}) reproduces the same boundary
    logic in a non-RF domain. When the label is wavepacket momentum,
    the information lives in the phase gradient and magnitude-only
    remains at chance. When the task is inverse potential
    classification after Schr\"odinger evolution, the task is
    learnable above chance but the best model is an explicit real
    Cartesian view, not the complex model. The pilot supports the
    application scope of representation stress testing; it does not
    convert the paper into a broad physics benchmark.
  \item \textbf{Empirical claim.} Complex-valued networks beat
    capacity-matched real-valued ones on synthetic IQ modulation
    classification (\Cref{sec:synthetic-rf}) and on a 3-class subset of
    RadioML 2018.01A~\cite{oshea2018radioml}
    (\Cref{sec:radioml}) under matched-budget evaluation, but the
    magnitude and interpretation of those wins are conditional on
    representation, SNR, activation, and invariance assumptions. On a
    task with no temporal phase structure (1-D phase classification,
    \Cref{sec:phase-classification}), the complex inductive bias offers
    no measurable advantage. Both positive and null results are
    reported.
  \item \textbf{Optimization claim.} The apparent magnitude of the
    RadioML win is activation-dependent
    (\Cref{sec:radioml-ablation}) because matched-shared-trial
    selection picks high-lr regimes under some activations that real
    baselines cannot tolerate. Per-step gradient telemetry
    (\Cref{sec:radioml-mechanism}) localizes the failure to a step-1
    head-gradient explosion that puts a fraction of real-baseline seeds
    into a dead-ReLU region they cannot escape; the threshold behavior
    replicates on synthetic data. A 2$\times$2 lr-vs-activation
    disambiguation (\Cref{sec:lr-vs-activation}) confirms the
    threshold is primarily lr-driven: at low lr both \code{crelu} and
    \code{zrelu} produce zero dead seeds; at high lr both produce
    them, with \code{crelu} more sensitive than \code{zrelu} ($4/9$
    vs $1/9$). Activation choice modulates the magnitude of the
    step-1 head explosion but does not by itself trigger the failure
    mode. Phase equivariance of the \emph{layer} is necessary
    for the architectural prior, but activation-level phase
    equivariance does not predict the empirical robustness asymmetry
    --- locating the asymmetry one level lower, in
    $(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$ parameter coupling at the optimization
    level, is what makes the picture self-consistent
    (\Cref{sec:phase-equivariance}).
  \item \textbf{Methodological claim.} The matched-shared-trial gap
    ($+22.94$~pp on \code{crelu}) and the independent-per-family gap
    ($+2.46$~pp on the same data) differ by an order of magnitude.
    We read matched-shared-trial as a robustness stress test and
    independent selection as the better point estimate of best
    achievable performance under separate tuning. Reporting both is
    part of the contribution; reporting only one risks overstating or
    erasing a real but small effect.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent We do \emph{not} claim:

\begin{itemize}
  \item That complex arithmetic is universally better than real-valued
    learning. The RF stress tests deliberately include cases where
    real magnitude or matched real baselines win. The contribution is
    to map the boundary of the advantage, not to erase the boundary.
  \item That CVNNs beat real-valued networks at full RadioML 2018.01A
    scale (24 modulations $\times$ 26 SNR levels $\times$ 1024-sample
    length). The infrastructure for that experiment is in place
    (\code{experiments/rf/sweep\_radioml.py --preset full}), but the
    run is deferred. The mechanism analysis is the contribution;
    scale-up is signposted future work.
  \item That the inductive bias survives in 2-D regimes (audio STFT,
    MRI). \code{ComplexConv2d} is not implemented; the project is 1-D
    only.
  \item That the quantum pilot establishes broad physics superiority.
    It is a deliberately small application probe over 1-D wavefunctions
    and five potential classes, included to test whether the
    representation thesis transfers outside RF.
  \item That CReLU activations generalize across signal-processing
    settings. The activation ablation
    (\Cref{sec:radioml-ablation}) is part of the contribution
    precisely because activation choice changes the appearance (though
    not the direction) of CVNN-vs-real gaps.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why feasibility-scale is the right scope}

A mechanism-level study requires the ability to (a) instrument every
parameter's gradient at every step, (b) run hundreds of training
configurations within a working budget, and (c) confirm cross-task
generalization with a second data distribution. All three are tractable
at the scope chosen --- 60 instrumented runs per data source, a
re-runnable budgeted sweep, a synthetic stand-in matching the real-data
benchmark on architecture and hyperparameter regime. They are not
tractable for paper deadlines at 24-class $\times$ 1024-sample
$\times$ 100-seed scale. We treat the smaller scope as a positive
feature: it lets us report what is happening, not just that something
is happening.

\subsection{Reproducibility as a first-class concern}

Every empirical claim above is backed by a committed result manifest
that records the Python / PyTorch / OS / device / dtype, every seed
used, the exact \code{git\_commit} of the producing code, and a
\code{git\_dirty} boolean. CI surfaces newly added dirty manifests so
they are reviewed deliberately rather than slipping through. A
structural CI guard checks that the activation characterization
artifact regenerates without errors and contains all six expected
activation rows. The companion repository currently has 139 passing
unit and integration tests, with two CUDA-specific tests skipped on the
local non-CUDA development machine.

\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

The infrastructure exists to answer the ``but what about\dots ?''
questions that ad-hoc CVNN comparisons in the literature routinely
sidestep.

\paragraph{CVNN library.}
We implement \code{ComplexLinear} (matmul + bias-add, deliberately
bypassing \code{F.linear} to sidestep its documented MPS gap),
\code{ComplexConv1d} (thin wrapper over \code{F.conv1d}, which natively
supports complex tensors on CPU/MPS/CUDA), and \code{ComplexDropout}
using a single shared real Bernoulli mask following Trabelsi
et~al.~\cite{trabelsi2018deep}. Six activations span the Liouville
trade-off: \code{CReLU}, \code{ZReLU}, \code{ModReLU}~\cite{arjovsky2016unitary},
\code{ComplexCardioid}, \code{Siglog}, and \code{ComplexTanh} as the
cautionary baseline. Initialization (Glorot~\cite{glorot2010understanding}
and Kaiming~\cite{he2015delving}) is complex-aware in both rectangular
and polar (Rayleigh-magnitude / uniform-phase) forms.

\paragraph{Activation characterization toolkit.}
For each candidate activation, we render a Cauchy--Riemann residual map
over the complex plane, compute summary statistics (median / p95 /
max), and measure two gradient norms: an MLP-scaffolded one and an
\emph{intrinsic} one ($|\partial L / \partial \bar{z}|$ for
$L = \sum |f(z)|^2$ on uniformly sampled $z$). The intrinsic measure
decouples the activation's contribution from any surrounding linear
layers --- without it, $\tanh$'s 855 MLP-gradient looks
$\sim$8$\times$ larger than its 108 intrinsic contribution.

\paragraph{Baseline matching.}
Every claim of ``complex beats real'' is backed by four families
running on the same task with the same shared search space:
\emph{naive real-stacked} (real net taking $(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$
as 2 channels; $\sim$2$\times$ the parameters of the complex layer);
\emph{matched-parameter real} (real net sized down so its real
\code{numel} equals the complex's, counting each complex slot as 2);
\emph{matched-FLOP real} (sized to match the complex MAdd count);
\emph{exact reparameterization} (the block-real $\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}
A & -B \\ B & A \end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$ construction proven to
reproduce the complex forward bit-for-bit; a witness, not a baseline to
beat).

\paragraph{Representation stress tests.}
For RF synthetic data we add a second axis of control: what information
the real network receives. In addition to Cartesian stacking
$(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$, the stress suite tests polar
$(|z|, \cos\theta, \sin\theta)$, phase-only
$(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$, and magnitude-only $|z|$ views. The same
runner probes PSK-only, QAM-only, mixed PSK+QAM, low- and high-SNR
regimes, unit-magnitude and unit-power normalization, fixed carrier
phase rotation, random-rotation augmentation, and complex activation
choice. This turns the research question from ``does complex beat
real?'' into the more falsifiable question ``which representation and
invariance assumptions explain the gap?''

\paragraph{Budgeted random search.}
Every model family draws from the \emph{same} shared search space and
consumes the same trial $\times$ seed budget. The sweep harness samples
the hyperparameter list once and runs it against each family in turn.
The primary matched comparison chooses the complex reference trial by
mean validation accuracy, then reports every real baseline at that
same trial index. Independent family winners are also written as
diagnostics but are not the matched-capacity paper table.

\section{Findings}
\label{sec:findings}

\subsection{Activation characterization}
\label{sec:activation-characterization}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/activation_tradeoff.png}
  \caption{Liouville trade-off, made empirical. \emph{Left}: gradient
    norm at init for each candidate activation, log scale.
    \emph{Right}: median and p95 Cauchy--Riemann residual on a
    $[-3, 3]^2$ grid in $\mathbb{C}$, log scale. \code{complex\_tanh}
    is locally holomorphic but unbounded (poles at $\pm i\pi/2$ inside
    the grid); \code{siglog} is bounded but never holomorphic;
    \code{crelu}, \code{zrelu}, \code{modrelu}, and
    \code{complex\_cardioid} sit between, each making a different
    compromise.}
  \label{fig:activation-tradeoff}
\end{figure}

\Cref{fig:activation-tradeoff} makes the Liouville bind empirically
visible. \code{complex\_tanh} has near-zero CR residual at the median
--- it really is locally holomorphic --- but the same metric's max is
905, because the grid (extent $= 3$) brackets the first pair of poles
at $z = \pm i\pi/2 \approx \pm 1.5708\,i$. The intrinsic Jacobian mean
is $\sim$108. By contrast, \code{siglog} is bounded ($|f| < 1$
everywhere) and never blows up, but its CR residual is everywhere
positive (p95 = 0.25) --- it is not holomorphic anywhere, by
construction. The remaining four activations sit between, each making
a different compromise.

This is not a finding about which activation is best in training ---
it is a visible measurement of the formal trade-off the project is
built around. The choice of activation in the downstream benchmarks
(default \code{crelu}) is consistent across runs and isolated from the
architectural variables.

\subsection{Synthetic phase classification --- null result}
\label{sec:phase-classification}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/synthetic_phase_classification_swept.png}
  \caption{1-D phase classification: matched shared-trial test
    accuracy after a 16$\times$3 sweep. All four families converge to
    $\sim$76\% with overlapping CIs --- the complex inductive bias has
    nothing to bite on at this scope.}
  \label{fig:phase-swept}
\end{figure}

After a 16$\times$3 budgeted sweep, the matched shared-trial comparison
puts all four families at $\sim$76.2--76.7\% test accuracy with
overlapping CIs (\Cref{fig:phase-swept}). On 1-D phase classification,
\textbf{the complex inductive bias does not help.} A 2-D real network
on $(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$ already has access to all the
information the task contains; there is no temporal or geometric
structure for $\mathbb{C}$-multiplication to exploit. The
\Cref{lem:phase-equivariance} of \Cref{sec:phase-equivariance} predicts
exactly this --- a single-sample phase task does not invoke any
sequence-level equivariance.

\subsection{RF representation stress tests --- where the claim breaks}
\label{sec:representation-stress}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/rf_stress_accuracy_heatmap.png}
  \caption{RF synthetic representation stress tests. Rows vary the
    modulation family, SNR regime, normalization, rotation shift, and
    complex activation; columns vary the model or real coordinate
    representation. The heatmap is useful precisely because it shows
    both wins and failures: complex wins PSK and mixed regimes, but
    magnitude wins QAM-only; fixed rotation collapses every

  \label{fig:rf-stress-heatmap}
\end{figure}

The matched-budget result in \Cref{sec:synthetic-rf} answers one
question: under a conventional Cartesian real baseline, does the
complex conv stack help? It does. But it does not answer the more
important mechanism question: is the advantage due to native complex
arithmetic, phase information, amplitude information, or a coordinate
choice that a real network could also receive? The representation
stress tests in \Cref{fig:rf-stress-heatmap} make those alternatives
explicit.

Three conclusions follow. First, PSK-only modulation is the cleanest
case for the complex prior: magnitude-only is exactly chance while
phase-aware real views and complex models learn. Second, the same
statement is false for QAM-only data: amplitude carries class
structure, and magnitude-only is the best single view in the committed
stress test. Third, native complex layers are not automatically
invariant to a carrier-phase offset: fixed rotation at test time drops
all coordinate-dependent PSK models to chance-level behavior, and
random rotation augmentation only partially restores performance.
This is the key scope refinement. The paper should not claim
``complex beats real'' as a universal result; it should claim that
complex-valued models are a useful inductive bias for RF signal
learning when the task's phase/amplitude geometry and invariance
assumptions match the model.

\subsection{Quantum wavefunction pilot --- physics application}
\label{sec:quantum-pilot}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/physics_quantum_accuracy_heatmap.png}
  \caption{1-D quantum wavefunction pilot. The momentum task stores the
    label in the phase gradient of $\psi(x)$, so magnitude-only stays
    at chance while phase-aware views solve it. The potential-inverse
    tasks are above chance but do not give a universal complex win:
    explicit real coordinate systems match or beat the complex model,
    and global-phase robustness must be tested directly.}
  \label{fig:quantum-pilot}
\end{figure}

To test whether the same representation story transfers outside RF, we
added a small physics pilot over 1-D complex wavefunctions. The data
are synthetic but physically grounded. In the first task, examples are
Gaussian wavepackets
$\psi(x)=A(x)\exp(i k x + i\phi_0)$ and the model predicts one of four
momentum classes. The amplitude envelope is randomized independently
of class, so the class information is in the phase gradient rather
than in $|\psi|$. In the second task, a right-moving wavepacket is
evolved under the time-dependent Schr\"odinger equation with a
split-step Fourier integrator, and the model predicts one of five
potential families: free, barrier, well, double barrier, or harmonic.
We also evaluate the potential task under an unseen global phase
rotation $\psi\mapsto i\psi$ and with random global-phase augmentation
at train time.

    thesis, not a broad complex-dominance claim.}
  \label{tab:quantum-pilot}

    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}

\end{table}

\Cref{tab:quantum-pilot} gives the useful scope boundary. First,
phase can be indispensable in a physics state: all phase-aware or
Cartesian views reach 1.000 on momentum classification, while
magnitude-only is exactly chance. This is the quantum analogue of the
PSK result. Second, the potential-inverse task is learnable above
chance, but the best model is the explicit real Cartesian baseline
(0.462) rather than the complex model (0.431). Third, global phase is a
physical nuisance symmetry, but the current complex conv stack does not
automatically solve it: under an unseen $\pi/2$ phase shift, complex
drops to 0.323 while magnitude remains unchanged at 0.331. Random
global-phase augmentation partially recovers the complex model
(0.395), but does not make this a solved invariance problem. The
physics pilot therefore strengthens the paper's positioning: complex
models are plausible for domains with native complex fields, but the
right claim is still representation- and symmetry-conditional.

\subsubsection{Selection rule matters: matched-shared-trial vs.\ independent}
\label{sec:selection-rules}

The single most important methodological caveat in this paper is that
the headline 22--23~pp gap on \code{crelu} / \code{cardioid} /
\code{siglog} is selection-rule-dependent. Re-doing the ablation with
\emph{independent per-family hyperparameter selection} --- each family
picks its own best of 16 trials, instead of all four families being
evaluated at the trial that complex selected --- collapses the
\code{crelu} comparison from \textbf{0.7293 vs 0.4999 (gap $+22.94$~pp,
matched-shared-trial)} to \textbf{0.7293 vs 0.7047 (gap $+2.46$~pp,
independent selection)}. The reduction comes from independent
selection allowing real baselines to avoid the high-lr trial that
triggers the step-1 explosion.

\subsubsection{Per-SNR breakdown}

At $-10$~dB everyone is near chance ($\sim$33\% for 3 classes); from
$+2$~dB onward, the \code{CReLU} complex network reaches 92.1\% at
$+10$~dB and 91.6\% at $+18$~dB while the real baselines plateau at

\subsubsection{Comparison to published RadioML numbers}

The RadioML 2018.01A dataset paper~\cite{oshea2018radioml} reports a
real-valued ResNet-style classifier reaching roughly 90\% top-1 at
high SNR on the full 24-class task with the full sample length 1024.
Subsequent CVNN-on-RadioML
work~\cite{krzyston2020complex,tu2020complex} reports a typical
complex-vs-real advantage of a few percentage points on the same
24-class setup with comparable architectures. Our $+3$-and-flat
ablation result on \code{modrelu} / \code{zrelu} (the activations under
which real baselines are also stable) is consistent with that small
reported gap. The 22--23~pp gap on \code{crelu} / \code{cardioid} /
\code{siglog} is \emph{not} a contradiction of the literature --- it
is the matched-shared-trial selection rule magnifying real-baseline
instability that the literature's separate-tuning protocols would not
expose.

\label{sec:radioml-mechanism}

The mechanism is unambiguous (\Cref{fig:telemetry-grad}). On
\code{crelu} / \code{cardioid} / \code{siglog}, matched-shared-trial
picks a high-lr / wide-hidden config that complex selects because it
tolerates it (lr $\in \{0.024, 0.024, 0.040\}$, hidden = 64). At
step~1, every family sees a large AdamW step away from random init.
For real baselines this produces a step-1 train loss in $[3, 36]$, a
total gradient norm in $[6, 42]$, and a \code{head.weight} gradient in
$[6, 41]$ --- i.e.\ nearly all of the explosion sits on the final
classifier's weights. For the complex network the same step produces a
step-1 loss in $[1.07, 1.28]$, total gradient norm $[0.3, 1.0]$,
\code{head.weight} gradient $[0.1, 0.7]$ --- about 50--80$\times$
smaller than its real counterparts on identical data and learning rate.

The complex network's $(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$ parameter coupling
distributes the loss signal across more parameter slots, so the same
$\mathrm{lr} \times \mathrm{CE\text{-}gradient}$ product produces a
smaller effective step on \code{head.weight}. The real network does
not have that smoothing; AdamW's first step pushes some seeds into a
region where the second \code{Conv1d}'s ReLU activations are all-zero
(or all-active and saturated), gradients vanish, and training
``stabilizes'' at uniform-prediction chance level. We confirmed this:
\emph{the dead seeds end at exactly $\mathrm{loss} = \ln 3 \approx
1.099$} (3-class CE under uniform predictions), with
\code{total\_grad\_norm} $\leq 0.05$, and zero test-accuracy
improvement from random init.

dead-seed rate jumps from 0\% to 33\% as soon as the
matched-shared-trial selected lr crosses $\sim$0.02.} Two orders of
magnitude separation in the step-1 head gradient between the unstable
and stable activation regimes.

the dominant axis.} Dropping \code{crelu} from its matched lr

activations cross from ``stable'' to ``unstable'' as lr crosses

not activation-driven, and replicates the

swapped in.

\code{crelu} produces a step-1 head gradient $\sim$5$\times$ larger
than \code{zrelu} ($31.4$ vs $6.3$) and four times the dead-seed rate
($4/9$ vs $1/9$); the split-ReLU structure of \code{crelu} amplifies
the readout's response to a high-lr first step but does not by itself
trigger the failure mode at safe learning rates. The honest reading
of \Cref{tab:dead-seeds} is therefore not ``some activations are
unstable and others are stable'' but ``the matched-shared-trial rule
selected unsafe learning rates for some activations and safe ones
for others, and that lr difference --- modulated by activation
sensitivity --- is what drives the dead-seed counts.''

\section{Interpretation}
\label{sec:interpretation}

The findings together support a more nuanced falsifiable story than
the original ``complex wins'' framing:

\begin{quote}
  Complex-valued models should be viewed as structured inductive
  biases for phase- and amplitude-bearing data, not as universally
  stronger function approximators. They help when the task's geometry
  aligns with complex multiplication, they can be matched or beaten by
  explicit real coordinate systems in other regimes, and they change
  optimization tolerance under shared hyperparameter stress.
\end{quote}

This framing is deliberately application-facing. In RF engineering, the
question is rarely whether the samples are ``complex'' in a formal
sense; they are. The practical question is whether the deployment
setting needs a model that preserves phase geometry, extracts amplitude
geometry, withstands unknown carrier-phase rotations, or trains
robustly under limited tuning. The stress tests show that those are
separate axes. A PSK classifier, a QAM classifier, and a rotated
carrier-offset classifier should not be expected to reward the same
architecture. The quantum pilot makes the same point for physics:
wavefunction momentum is invisible to $|\psi|$ but explicit
Cartesian/polar coordinates can match native complex layers, and
global phase has to be encoded as a symmetry assumption rather than
assumed from the dtype.

\subsection{Why complex helps on IQ data --- phase equivariance}
\label{sec:phase-equivariance}

The intuition that ``complex multiplication has the right semantics
for phase-bearing signals'' can be made formal in one short lemma.

\begin{lemma}[Phase equivariance of \code{ComplexConv1d}]
  \label{lem:phase-equivariance}
  Let $C(z)[t] = \sum_{\tau} K[\tau] \cdot z[t-\tau] + b$ be a
  \code{ComplexConv1d} layer with complex-valued kernel $K$, complex
  bias $b$, and complex input sequence $z$. For any global phase
  $\varphi \in \mathbb{R}$ and any $t$,
  \[
    C(e^{i\varphi} \cdot z)[t]
    = e^{i\varphi} \cdot C(z)[t] + (1 - e^{i\varphi}) \cdot b.
  \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  By linearity of complex multiplication and convolution,
  \[
    C(e^{i\varphi} \cdot z)[t]
    = \sum_{\tau} K[\tau] \cdot (e^{i\varphi} \cdot z[t-\tau]) + b
    = e^{i\varphi} \sum_{\tau} K[\tau] \cdot z[t-\tau] + b
    = e^{i\varphi} \cdot \bigl(C(z)[t] - b\bigr) + b. \qedhere
  \]
\end{proof}

In the bias-free case (or after subtracting the bias) the result is
exactly equivariant. The biased case adds a fixed offset
$(1 - e^{i\varphi}) \cdot b$. If the intervening stack preserves the
global phase action, the head's $|\cdot|$ magnitude operation removes
it ($|e^{i\varphi} \cdot y| = |y|$ for real $\varphi$), yielding
phase-invariant logits. Our actual benchmarks include activations that
do \emph{not} preserve exact equivariance, so we treat this as an
architectural prior supplied by complex multiplication rather than a
guarantee that the trained network is globally phase-invariant.

The same does not hold for \code{Conv1d} operating on a stacked
$(\mathrm{Re}\,z, \mathrm{Im}\,z)$ real input. A global phase shift
$e^{i\varphi}$ corresponds to multiplying each $(I, Q)$ sample by the
$2 \times 2$ rotation matrix $R_\varphi$. To match
\code{ComplexConv1d}'s equivariance, every real conv kernel would need
to commute with $R_\varphi$ applied to its 2 input channels --- a
constraint real \code{Conv1d} does not impose by construction. The
real network \emph{can} learn the symmetry from data, but it has to
spend capacity on it; the complex network gets it for free.

This is the structural inductive bias the synthetic phase
classification result (\Cref{sec:phase-classification}), the RF
stress tests (\Cref{sec:representation-stress}), and the RadioML result
(\Cref{sec:radioml}) probe in complementary regimes. A single complex
sample (phase classification) gives no temporal structure for
convolutional equivariance to bite on, and the 2-D real network has
the same ``see real and imag'' feature view, so the complex network
buys nothing --- the null is what \Cref{lem:phase-equivariance}
predicts. A length-$L$ IQ sequence does have temporal structure, but
\Cref{sec:representation-stress} shows that temporal structure alone
is not enough to justify a universal claim: PSK rewards phase-aware
geometry, QAM rewards amplitude geometry, and fixed carrier-phase
rotation requires either augmentation or an explicit invariance
mechanism. The observed wins are consistent with the complex prior
when these conditions match, without requiring every activation in the
stack to preserve exact equivariance.

\paragraph{What the lemma does not explain.}
Phase equivariance of the \emph{layer} is necessary for the
architectural prior, but the activations between conv layers vary in
their own equivariance. By direct computation: \code{modrelu} and
\code{siglog} are phase-equivariant; \code{crelu}, \code{cardioid},
and \code{zrelu} are not. If the \Cref{sec:radioml} robustness
asymmetry were \emph{driven} by activation phase equivariance, the
stable activations should be exactly \code{modrelu} and \code{siglog}.
They are not: \Cref{sec:radioml-mechanism} shows \code{modrelu} and
\code{zrelu} are stable ($0/9$ dead seeds across nine real-baseline
runs each), while \code{crelu}, \code{cardioid}, and \code{siglog} are
unstable ($3/9$ dead). One phase-equivariant activation is on each
side of the divide. The \Cref{sec:lr-vs-activation} disambiguation
sharpens this further: when we swap learning rates between
\code{crelu} and \code{zrelu}, the dead-seed pattern follows the lr,
not the activation. \textbf{Phase equivariance of the layer is
necessary for the inductive bias, but the robustness asymmetry
\Cref{sec:radioml-mechanism} measures lives one level lower --- at
the optimization dynamics induced by $(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$
parameter coupling on the classifier head under high-lr AdamW steps,
not at the equivariance of the activation.} The two effects are
partially independent and partially compounding. This also explains why the
\Cref{sec:radioml} result is most defensible at activations like
\code{zrelu} where complex wins by a small margin rather than at
\code{crelu} where the apparent gap is amplified by the
optimization-side mechanism.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RadioML subset, not the full archive.} The headline
    real-data run used BPSK / QPSK / 8PSK at 8 SNR levels with
    \code{max\_per\_class\_per\_snr=256}. The full archive has 24
    modulations $\times$ 26 SNRs and a much larger per-bucket count.
  \item \textbf{Synthetic QAM is measured, RadioML QAM is not.} The
    stress tests include QAM16 / QAM64 and show that magnitude can
    dominate there, but the real-data RadioML mechanism study remains
    PSK-only. A full RadioML QAM/mixed-modulation run is required
    before making application claims about amplitude-heavy real RF
    classes.
  \item \textbf{No learned rotation invariance guarantee.} The fixed
    rotation stress test shows that neither complex layers nor
    phase-aware real features automatically solve unseen carrier-phase
    offsets under the current architecture. Rotation augmentation helps
    but does not make the problem disappear.
  \item \textbf{Physics pilot is deliberately small.} The quantum
    wavefunction study uses 1-D synthetic states, a short split-step
    evolution, five hand-designed potential families, and three seeds.
    It is evidence that the representation/stress-test protocol
    transfers outside RF, not evidence about production scientific ML
    workloads.
  \item \textbf{Six seeds.} CIs are computed from a Gaussian
    approximation of the per-seed mean ($\sigma/\sqrt{n}$). A
    bootstrap CI on this many seeds gives similar widths but is not
    substantially more honest.
  \item \textbf{One loss.} Classification runs use cross-entropy.
  \item \textbf{No \code{ComplexBatchNorm} or \code{ComplexLayerNorm}.}
    Landing them would let us train deeper conv stacks fairly.
  \item \textbf{Modest hardware.} CPU and Apple Silicon for
    development; one A100 for the headline RF sweep. No
    multi-machine reproducibility check.
  \item \textbf{Lr-vs-activation disambiguation is 2$\times$2.}
    \Cref{sec:lr-vs-activation} establishes the lr-driven character
    of the dead-seed threshold using \code{crelu} (the largest gap)
    and \code{zrelu} (the smallest), but does not run the full
    factorial across all five activations. The cardioid and siglog
    rows in \Cref{tab:dead-seeds} are unaccounted for under this
    framing except by interpolation; a five-activation lr sweep
    would close that gap.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future-work}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Scale up the RadioML run} to the full
    24-modulation $\times$ 26-SNR archive on the full 1024-sample
    length. The infrastructure is in place
    (\code{sweep\_radioml.py --preset full}); the run is deferred from
    the headline write-up to keep the paper anchored on the 3-class
    subset that supports the mechanism analysis. Recommended first
    activation is \code{zrelu} --- the only ablation point where real
    baselines also train cleanly, so the comparison is least confounded
    by the matched-shared-trial selection's interaction with the
    explosion mechanism.
  \item \textbf{Port the representation stress suite to RadioML.}
    The synthetic stress tests are already the right scientific shape:
    PSK-only, QAM-only, mixed modulations, low/high SNR, amplitude
    normalization, carrier-phase rotation, augmentation, and activation
    ablation. The next defensible scale-up is to run the same grid on
    RadioML rather than only increasing class count.
  \item \code{ComplexBatchNorm} (whitening 2$\times$2 covariance, per
    Trabelsi et al.~\cite{trabelsi2018deep}) and deeper conv stacks.
  \item \textbf{Conv2d and 2-D complex domains.} Audio STFT, SAR,
    MRI, and spectrogram-like RF features are natural targets, but they
    require \code{ComplexConv2d}, normalization, and representation
    stress tests specific to 2-D phase/amplitude geometry.
  \item \textbf{Scale the physics pilot} from 1-D wavefunctions to
    richer PDE settings: 2-D wave packets, inverse scattering,
    Fourier-domain operators, and explicit gauge/global-phase
    invariant architectures.
  \item A separate study of per-activation training dynamics: does
    \code{ModReLU}'s bias-sign regime visibly affect convergence?
\end{itemize}

\section{Phase equivariance: full proof and caveats}
\label{app:phase-equivariance}

\Cref{lem:phase-equivariance} states that a bias-free
\code{ComplexConv1d} layer commutes with global phase rotation, and
that the bias-bearing case picks up a controlled correction term. We
expand the proof and discuss what does and does not propagate through
the activation stack.

\begin{proposition}[Phase equivariance, bias-free]
\label{prop:phase-equivariance-biasfree}
Let $C : \mathbb{C}^{T} \to \mathbb{C}^{T}$ denote a one-dimensional
complex convolution with kernel $w \in \mathbb{C}^{K}$, no bias, and
zero padding (\code{padding\_mode='zeros'}). For any
$z \in \mathbb{C}^{T}$ and any global phase $\phi \in \mathbb{R}$:
\[
  C(e^{i\phi} z)[t] \;=\; e^{i\phi} \, C(z)[t]
  \qquad \forall t \in \{0, \ldots, T-1\}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By linearity over $\mathbb{C}$:
\[
  C(e^{i\phi} z)[t]
  = \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} w_k \cdot (e^{i\phi} z)[t-k]
  = e^{i\phi} \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} w_k \cdot z[t-k]
  = e^{i\phi} \, C(z)[t].
\]
The phase factor commutes through the sum because complex scalar
multiplication is commutative; the sum extracts it cleanly. Boundary
indices $t - k < 0$ contribute zero on both sides because zero padding
is itself phase-invariant ($e^{i\phi} \cdot 0 = 0$). \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Phase equivariance, bias-bearing]
\label{prop:phase-equivariance-bias}
With learnable bias $b \in \mathbb{C}$:
\[
  C(e^{i\phi} z)[t] \;=\; e^{i\phi} \, C(z)[t] \;+\; (1 - e^{i\phi}) \, b.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$C(e^{i\phi} z)[t] = e^{i\phi} (C(z)[t] - b) + b = e^{i\phi} C(z)[t] +
b - e^{i\phi} b$. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What survives the activation stack.} Phase equivariance is
preserved layer-by-layer if and only if the activation $\sigma$ itself
satisfies $\sigma(e^{i\phi} z) = e^{i\phi} \sigma(z)$.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ModReLU} ($\sigma(z) = \mathrm{ReLU}(|z| + b) \cdot
    e^{i \angle z}$): equivariant. The magnitude term depends only on
    $|z|$, which is rotation-invariant; the phase factor passes through
    unchanged.
  \item \textbf{ComplexCardioid} ($\sigma(z) = \tfrac{1}{2}
    (1 + \cos \angle z) \cdot z$): \emph{not} equivariant in general,
    because the gate changes under rotation:
    $\cos(\angle z + \phi) \neq \cos(\angle z)$ for generic $\phi$.
  \item \textbf{Siglog} ($\sigma(z) = z / (1 + |z|)$): equivariant.
  \item \textbf{ComplexTanh} (split $\tanh$ on real/imag parts):
    \emph{not} equivariant. A rotation $e^{i\phi}$ mixes real and
    imaginary components before the elementwise nonlinearity.
  \item \textbf{CReLU} (split ReLU on real/imag): \emph{not}
    equivariant; same reason as ComplexTanh.
  \item \textbf{ZReLU} (zero out unless $\angle z \in [0, \pi/2]$): not
    equivariant (the kept-quadrant condition is rotation-sensitive).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{So four of our six characterized activations break equivariance.} The
empirical robustness asymmetry we report (a step-1
explosion-into-dead-region pattern that disproportionately hurts real
baselines) does \emph{not} track which activations preserve layer-level
phase equivariance. CReLU does not preserve equivariance but is the
configuration with the largest measured CVNN-vs-real gap; Siglog does
preserve equivariance but appears in the unstable high-lr regime. We
therefore locate the asymmetry one level lower than the activation, in the
$(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$ parameter coupling that the optimizer sees
on \code{head.weight} at step 1; \Cref{sec:radioml-mechanism}
develops this in the body.

selection rules is itself one of the artifacts the C\&F framing
exposes.

\section{Architecture details and parameter counts}
\label{app:architectures}

The four baseline families differ in capacity-allocation rule against
the complex network:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{complex}: \code{ComplexConv1d(2c$\to$h)} or
    \code{ComplexLinear(d$\to$h)}, complex weights \texttt{[h$\times$2c, K]} or
    \texttt{[h$\times$d]} with 2 floats/parameter (real and imaginary).
  \item \code{real\_stacked}: \code{Conv1d(2$\to$h)} or
    \code{Linear(2d$\to$h)}; treats $(I, Q)$ as 2 input channels.
    Same $h$ as the complex network, half the parameter count
    (1 float per weight).
  \item \code{real\_matched\_params}: hidden width chosen to match
    parameter count to the complex family. For a complex
    \code{(2c $\to$ h, K)} layer with $2chK$ complex weights = $4chK$
    floats, the matched real layer is \code{(2 $\to$ 2h, K)} = $4hK$
    floats, identical at $c=1$ but scaling to $\sqrt{2} h$ at $c > 1$.
  \item \code{real\_matched\_flops}: hidden width chosen to match
    forward-pass multiply-accumulate count rather than parameter count.
    For our 1-D conv layers the resulting widths track
    \code{matched\_params} closely (within 5\%); listed separately to
    avoid silently conflating ``capacity'' senses.
\end{itemize}

The synthetic phase classifier uses a 1-layer MLP head (no conv); the
RF/RadioML classifiers use \code{ComplexConv1d} (or \code{Conv1d} on
$(I,Q)$ stacked channels) followed by global magnitude pool + linear
readout. Magnitude pool is $|z|$ per channel for the complex family
and identity for real, since the real channels are already real-valued
at the readout.

\section{Gradient telemetry: per-layer, per-step}
\label{app:telemetry}

Telemetry instruments every parameter tensor with per-step
$\|\nabla_\theta L\|_F$, the Cauchy-Riemann residual on complex
parameters (zero by construction for the complex family), and an
intrinsic Jacobian-norm proxy. Runs cover 5 activations
$\times$ 4 families $\times$ 3 seeds = 60 instrumented trajectories,
each capped at 200 steps for tractability on commodity hardware.

\paragraph{Step-1 head-gradient ratio.} Across all five activations,
the step-1 ratio
$\|\nabla_{\text{head.weight}} L\|_{\text{real}} \,/\,
\|\nabla_{\text{head.weight}} L\|_{\text{complex}}$
falls into two regimes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Gap-bearing} (CReLU, ComplexCardioid, Siglog): ratio
    in the range $50$--$80$ at step 1; collapses to $\sim 1$ by step
    20 for surviving seeds, but seeds whose post-step-1 head landed in
    a dead-ReLU region never produce gradient signal again.
  \item \textbf{Stable} (ModReLU, ZReLU): ratio in the range
    $1.5$--$3$ at step 1; no seeds enter dead-loss states.
\end{itemize}

The grouping is consistent across seeds and across the synthetic-data
replication (\Cref{app:synthetic-replication}). The
$50$--$80\times$ figure is a step-1 measurement on raw weights with
fan-in initialization; later steps post-Adam-warmup show smaller
ratios under all activations. The point of measuring at step 1 is that
that is where the dead seeds become dead; past step 1, the parameter
state has already diverged.

\paragraph{Why \code{head.weight}, specifically.} The conv stack's
parameters show no comparable real-vs-complex ratio at any step. The
explosion is a property of how matched-trial hyperparameter selection
maps into the readout when the readout sees magnitude-pooled inputs
(complex) versus stacked $(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$ inputs (real).
The complex magnitude pool produces inputs of comparable scale across
seeds; the stacked $(\mathrm{Re}, \mathrm{Im})$ readout sees inputs
whose scale is seed-dependent through the conv-stack random init, and
high-lr trials interact badly with the unlucky tail of that
distribution.

\section{Synthetic-data replication of the threshold mechanism}
\label{app:synthetic-replication}

To rule out RadioML-specific effects (CFO, multipath, pulse shaping)
as the cause of the explosion, we re-run the gradient telemetry
harness on synthetic AWGN-only IQ data with the same 4-family / 5-act
sweep and the same matched-trial hyperparameters. The synthetic data
generator is \code{experiments/rf/synthetic\_modulation.py}, which
produces raw-symbol $(I, Q)$ sequences with per-class label imbalance
matched to RadioML.

\paragraph{Result.} The dead-seed pattern replicates: CReLU,
ComplexCardioid, and Siglog produce 2--3 dead seeds per 9 in the real
families; ModReLU and ZReLU produce zero. The step-1 head-gradient
ratio sits in the same $50$--$80\times$ band for the gap-bearing
activations. We conclude the mechanism is hyperparameter-driven,
mediated by the readout's input distribution under matched
shared-trial selection, and not an artifact of RadioML's distortions.

\section{RadioML 2018.01A loader: aliases and strict-SNR mode}
\label{app:radioml-loader}

\paragraph{Class-label aliasing.} The original
\code{classes.txt} distributed with RadioML 2018.01A lists the 24
modulations in an order that does not match the one-hot ordering of
the \code{Y} array; this is a known issue in the community.
\code{experiments/rf/radioml.py} normalizes class labels through a
canonical map and prefers \code{classes-fixed.json} or
\code{classes-fixed.txt} sidecars next to the HDF5 file when present.
Common aliases accepted: \code{PSK8}~$\to$~\code{8PSK},
\code{QAM16}~$\to$~\code{16QAM}, \code{APSK16}~$\to$~\code{16APSK},
\code{ASK4}~$\to$~\code{4ASK}, AM labels with underscores
(\code{AM\_SSB\_WC}~$\to$~\code{AM-SSB-WC}), etc.

\paragraph{Strict-SNR mode.} Earlier loader versions silently dropped
samples at SNR levels not in the user's filter list. The default is
now \code{strict\_snr=True}, which raises rather than dropping; the
test suite includes a regression case that asserts on the strict
behaviour. The previous silent-drop behaviour is recoverable via
\code{strict\_snr=False} for compatibility with downstream code that
intentionally subsets.

\label{app:reproducibility}

Every long-running script writes a JSON manifest into the run's output
directory (under \code{results/}). Manifest fields:

\begin{itemize}

\section{Full-archive RadioML scale-up: cost and deferred plan}
\label{app:scale-up}

The submitted experiments are at \code{--preset subset}: 3 PSK
modulations, 8 even SNR levels, \code{max\_per\_class\_per\_snr=256},
\code{sample\_length=128}. The infrastructure for full-archive runs is
in place via \code{--preset full} (24 modulations, 26 SNR levels,
\code{sample\_length=1024}); we have deliberately deferred running it
because the mechanism analysis is the contribution and the scale-up
risks scope creep relative to a C\&F submission.

\paragraph{Cost estimate.} A single
\code{--preset full --activation crelu --seeds 0 1 2} run on an A100
40GB takes 5--15 hours depending on which trial samples land at the
upper end of the search space (the search box's largest configuration
is the bottleneck). Six activations $\times$ 3 seeds $\times$ 16
trials $\times$ 4 families = 1152 instrumented runs at full scale; in
practice we would run 2--3 activations (CReLU as the headline,
ZReLU as the stable counter-example, and one of
\{ComplexCardioid, Siglog\} as a disambiguation). On 4$\times$A100 in
parallel, $\sim$24 hours wall-clock for a 3-activation full sweep is a
realistic estimate.

\paragraph{What to expect at scale.} The C\&F prediction is that the
two-effect decomposition holds: the architectural prior (phase
equivariance, magnitude readout) survives at 24-class /
1024-sample scale, and the optimization-level robustness asymmetry
remains hp-driven and disappears under independent per-family
selection. We do not predict the specific numerical gap because the
full-archive distribution is qualitatively different from the subset
(more distortion-heavy modulations like AM-SSB-WC and FM are present
at low SNR), and the matched-trial selection rule may admit different
hyperparameter regimes once the conv stack has more channels.

\paragraph{Failure modes to watch for.} (1) Caching off may surface
stragglers if the loader's \code{strict\_snr=True} default flags
HDF5 SNR levels not in the user's filter; the loader's regression
test covers this on the subset but the full archive has not been run
in CI. (2) A 24-class softmax has higher entropy at chance ($\ln 24
\approx 3.18$ vs $\ln 3 \approx 1.10$), so the dead-loss threshold for
detecting failed seeds shifts; the telemetry harness already infers
this from the dataset's class count.
